What this covers

Moving how card and card-reader payments settle for any tour departing 2027 or later — from an automatic Stripe destination-charge split to an explicit, code-driven Transfer to the operator’s connected account. Same guest experience, same operator payout timing, same pricing; only the underlying settlement mechanism and its visibility change.

Reading This Timeline

Assumptions & notes

What this schedule does and doesn’t assume, stated plainly.

Assumptions behind the 12-week estimate

  • Team size: roughly 2–3 engineers, with tasks inside a milestone split across people where the work is genuinely parallel (e.g. Milestone 3’s eleven checkout flows, Milestone 4’s nine refund paths).
  • Task estimates are engineering effort in person-days, not elapsed calendar time — milestone durations account for parallelization, review, and integration overhead on top of the raw effort sum.
  • Milestones are sequential: each depends on the work before it (the transfer engine needs the payment-mode gate; every checkout flow needs the transfer engine; refunds need the checkout flows wired first).
  • No 2027-dated tour has been sold yet, so this entire timeline can run in the background with zero risk to a real guest or booking already in progress.

What could move the date

  • Fewer than 2–3 engineers available, or engineers splitting time with other work, extends Milestones 3 and 4 the most — they carry the bulk of the effort.
  • Milestone 5 (Reporting) can start in parallel with the tail end of Milestone 4 if resourcing allows, tightening the schedule by up to a week.
  • A real Stripe-account or connected-account issue surfaced during Milestone 6’s rehearsal would extend that milestone specifically, not the ones before it.
